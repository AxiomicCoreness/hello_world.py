\section{Problem}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Minimise
\begin{equation}
  J(u) \;=\; \Phi(s(T)) \;+\; \int_0^T \! L(s,u)\,dt,
  \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}^{4d},
\end{equation}
subject to the hybrid dynamics of the adjoining manifold document:
free flow $\dot s = f(s,u)$ between impacts, and reset
$s^+ = g(s^-)$ at impact times $\gamma_k$ satisfying
$\psi(s(\gamma_k)) = 0$ with $\nabla\psi^\top f < 0$ (transversality).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Frozen sign convention (AMENDMENT 1)}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{convention}[Sign of the TOI sensitivity]
\label{conv:sign}
The implicit-differentiation identity is taken with its minus sign
NOT absorbed:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\gamma_k}{\partial u}
  \;=\;
  -\,\frac{\nabla\psi^\top \tfrac{\partial s(\gamma_k^-)}{\partial u}}
          {\nabla\psi^\top f}\Big|_{\gamma_k}.
\end{equation}
This is the same convention as the manifold document (toi_manifold,
$\partial\gamma_k/\partial u$ identity). The forward transport term
is therefore a projection:
\begin{equation}
  f \otimes \frac{\partial\gamma_k}{\partial u}
  \;=\;
  -\,f\,\frac{\nabla\psi^\top \tfrac{\partial s}{\partial u}}
             {\nabla\psi^\top f},
\end{equation}
and the adjoint jump (Proposition~\ref{prop:jump}) consumes the SAME
sign. Any future file that redefines $\partial\gamma_k/\partial u$
with the sign absorbed must be flagged against this convention; a
sign flip at the impact is a defect, not a style choice. Both files
reference this convention block.
\end{convention}

\section{The adjoint states}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{definition}[Continuous-phase adjoint]
Between impacts, the adjoint $(\lambda, \mu)$ obeys the standard
ODEs of the Pontryagin maximum principle:
\begin{align}
  \dot\lambda &= -\Bigl(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial s}\Bigr)^\top \lambda
                 \;-\; \nabla_s L, \\
  \dot\mu     &= -\Bigl(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial u}\Bigr)^\top \lambda
                 \;-\; \nabla_u L,
\end{align}
with terminal conditions $\lambda(T) = \nabla\Phi(s(T))$, $\mu(T) = 0$.
On any interval $(\gamma_k, \gamma_{k+1})$ the reduced gradient is
$\partial J/\partial u = \mu$ evaluated on that interval.
\end{definition}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The jump map of the adjoint (AMENDED)}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Naive hybrid adjoints carry only the Jacobian factor
$(\partial g/\partial s)^\top$ across the impact. That is wrong
whenever $\partial\gamma_k/\partial u \neq 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Adjoint jump at an impact]
\label{prop:jump}
Under Convention~\ref{conv:sign}, integrating the adjoint backward
across $\gamma_k$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(\gamma_k^-) \;=\;
  \Bigl(\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial s}\Bigr)^\top \lambda(\gamma_k^+)
  \;-\;
  \frac{\nabla\psi}{\nabla\psi^\top f}\,
    f^\top \Bigl(\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial s}\Bigr)^\top
    \lambda(\gamma_k^+)
  \;+\;
  \beta_{\mathrm{raw}} \, \nabla\psi,
\end{equation}
or equivalently, absorbing the projection term into a single
multiplier (AMENDMENT 2 — the file's declared convention):
\begin{equation}
  \lambda(\gamma_k^-) \;=\;
  \Bigl(\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial s}\Bigr)^\top \lambda(\gamma_k^+)
  \;+\;
  \beta_k \, \nabla\psi\bigl(s(\gamma_k)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \beta_k \;=\;
  \beta_{\mathrm{raw}}
  \;-\;
  \frac{f^\top\bigl(\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial s}\bigr)^\top
        \lambda(\gamma_k^+)}
       {\nabla\psi^\top f}\Big|_{\gamma_k}.
\end{equation}
Both forms are correct under their stated conventions; this file
DECLARES the absorbed form ($\beta_k$) as canonical. A reader who
computes $\beta_{\mathrm{raw}}$ and substitutes it for $\beta_k$ will
get a different $\lambda(\gamma_k^-)$ — the two are related by the
stated equation, not interchangeable. The control-side jump is
\begin{equation}
  \mu(\gamma_k^-) \;=\;
  \mu(\gamma_k^+) \;+\;
  \beta_k\,
  \frac{\nabla\psi^\top \tfrac{\partial s}{\partial u}}
       {\nabla\psi^\top f}\Big|_{\gamma_k},
\end{equation}
consistent with the transpose action of the forward transport term
under Convention~\ref{conv:sign}.
\end{proposition}

The structure: the $\beta_k\,\nabla\psi$ term is the adjoint image
of the forward transport term. Forward: $f \otimes
\partial\gamma_k/\partial u$ moves the impact time; backward:
$\beta_k\,\nabla\psi$ moves the impact constraint. Both arise from
the same differentiated manifold condition $\psi(s(\gamma_k))=0$.
Dropping $\beta_k$ is the adjoint false witness: a backward pass
that omits the multiplier reproduces the published failure
(pre-collision control decreasing when it should increase).

\section{Discrete form (AMENDED — transpose claim qualified)}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
For the discretised problem, the discrete adjoint obtained by
reverse-mode autodiff is the exact transpose of the IMPLEMENTED
forward computation — always, for any implementation, including
branching. That is a property of the autodiff machinery (CLAIM 1).

Whether the implemented forward computation is the correct forward
SENSITIVITY of the hybrid system is a separate, implementation-
conditional claim (CLAIM 2). The impact time $\gamma_k$ is
$C^0$ but not $C^1$ in the state at the crossing. A handler that
computes $\partial\gamma_k/\partial u$ by implicit differentiation
of $\psi = 0$ (Convention~\ref{conv:sign}) implements the correct
sensitivity, and reverse-mode autodiff then reproduces the
analytical adjoint jump of Proposition~\ref{prop:jump}. A smoothed
or clamped handler — fixed $\gamma$, smooth surrogate for the
crossing — implements a DIFFERENT function: autograd transposes it
exactly, but the result does not match the analytical jump.

\textbf{Correct statement:} in a correctly-implemented event
handler — one that computes $\partial\gamma_k/\partial u$ by
implicit differentiation of $\psi=0$ — reverse-mode autodiff
reproduces the analytical adjoint. The two agree at the level of
the correctly-implemented computation. They do NOT agree for
smoothed or clamped surrogates. The ``one object, not two''
identity is a consequence of the implementation matching the
analytical form, not a free property of autograd. A reader with a
clamped handler whose autograd adjoint disagrees with the
analytical jump should conclude the HANDLER is wrong, not the
analytical form.

$\{$A1, A2, A3$\}$; the benchmark additionally asserts A4a.

\section{Floating-base extension}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
For the legged robot the same jump applies at each touch-down,
with the manifold condition supplied by the unilateral foot
constraint and $g$ by the contact-impulse solution of
\begin{equation}
  M(q)\bigl(\ddot q + [0;g;0]\bigr) + n(q,\dot q)
  = u + \sum_i C_i(q)^\top f_i ,
\end{equation}
so the adjoint multiplier $\beta_k$ attaches to the foot-contact
constraint gradient, exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:jump}
prescribes for balls. Every footstep is a TOI event; the adjoint
jump is the per-footstep correction.
